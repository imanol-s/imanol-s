## The problem

Training on physical hardware makes every failed trajectory costly. Resets take time, and obstacle avoidance needs many interactions. Simulation gave the team a way to iterate before physical deployment.

## My contribution

On the Fall 2025 senior capstone team at UT Dallas, I built **simulation environments and reinforcement-learning training loops** for a mobile robot's navigation policy.

I recreated real-world environments in NVIDIA IsaacSim and helped define the IsaacLab training setup:

- What the robot observes.
- Which actions it can take.
- How actions are rewarded.
- When a trial ends.

The deliverable was a **simulation and training setup** for running repeated navigation trials.

## The approach

The team used Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for continuous control. IsaacSim provided the physics environment; IsaacLab organized training and parallel episodes on GPU.

The work also considered how continuous policy outputs would map to motor commands on the robot. That hardware-software boundary made eventual deployment validation an important next step.

## What I learned

Building the environment meant learning unfamiliar tools and deciding what the robot should observe and be rewarded for. Those choices were central to the navigation work, alongside the training algorithm. The work also made clear why a policy trained in simulation still needs validation on physical hardware.
