## The navigation task

Our capstone task was to teach a legged ground robot to follow an edge for outdoor maintenance. Simulation let us develop and evaluate navigation behavior before physical deployment.

On the Fall 2025 capstone team at UT Dallas, I was a **developer focused on implementation and documentation**, working on the simulation environments, reinforcement-learning training setup, and validation workflows.

## Training-task implementation

I recreated real-world environments in NVIDIA IsaacSim and helped define the IsaacLab training setup, including what the robot observes and which actions it can take.

I implemented task logic in Python:

- **Custom reward functions and penalties** supplied the feedback used to train edge-following behavior.
- **Termination conditions** defined when a training episode ends.

The team used Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for continuous control. IsaacSim provided the physics environment; IsaacLab organized the training loop. My contribution focused on the environments and task logic around the algorithm.

## Evaluation

I designed and trained policies through iterative experimentation. I analyzed simulation performance data and created visualizations to evaluate agent behavior across varying terrain conditions and edge-following scenarios.

I also implemented **scenario checks and scripted test runs** for simulation-based validation, providing repeatable ways to evaluate the resulting behavior.

## Documentation and integration

I documented the architecture connecting perception inputs, navigation logic, and motor control, creating technical references and onboarding materials for continued development.

That documentation described the hardware-software boundary: continuous policy outputs need to map to commands the physical robot can execute. The simulation work supported development and evaluation; it did not replace physical deployment validation.

## Deliverables and availability

My deliverables combined executable training-task logic, simulated environments, evaluation scripts, performance visualizations, and architecture documentation. Together, they supported the team's navigation experiments and provided references for continued development.

Source code and detailed results are covered under NDA and are not publicly available.
